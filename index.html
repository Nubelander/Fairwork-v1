Our Objective
What we're
trying to understand.

The debate over AI and work is loud but often evidence-light. FAIRWORK exists to change that — systematically coding the academic literature, surveying key stakeholders, and mapping the geographic and policy landscape of AI-driven labor market change.

01 / Document
Build the evidence base
Systematically review and code 200+ peer-reviewed papers on AI and labor markets, tracking exposure estimates, outcomes, methods, and industries across time.
02 / Analyze
Examine policy responses
Map regulatory and legislative responses across 12 countries, testing which interventions have evidence behind them and which are still theoretical.
03 / Train
Develop the next generation
Involve undergraduate and graduate researchers in real systematic review work — building skills in evidence synthesis, causal inference, and open science.

How We Work
Rigorous, transparent,
replicable research.
Step 01
Search & Screen
Database search across EBSCO, Web of Science, and Google Scholar using a pre-registered PRISMA protocol. Duplicates removed, titles and abstracts screened for inclusion criteria.
Step 02
Independent Coding
Two coders independently assign values to 40+ variables per paper — methodology, geography, industry, exposure measure, and outcome framing.
Step 03
Reconcile & Validate
Disagreements resolved by consensus or a third reviewer. Inter-rater reliability tracked continuously (Cohen's κ = 0.81 across current corpus).
Step 04
Analyze & Visualize
Coded data analyzed for patterns in exposure estimates, framing trends, geographic and sectoral gaps, and policy correlates. Results visualized in our public dashboard.
Step 05
Publish Everything
Full codebook, raw data, and analysis code released under CC-BY 4.0. Pre-registration posted before analysis. No selective reporting.
Commitment to Transparency

All hypotheses are pre-registered before data collection. Our full codebook, inter-rater reliability logs, and raw datasets are publicly available. We flag where findings are preliminary or contested.

What our data can — and can't — tell you

Our SLR reflects published academic literature, which skews toward measurable occupations and English-language sources. Exposure estimates show correlation with disruption risk — not guaranteed displacement. We flag contested findings explicitly.

Who Is It For?
Policymakers
Evidence to ground AI workforce policy in what research actually shows, not projections or anecdote.
  • Benchmark national AI policy against international responses
  • Access exposure data disaggregated by region and sector
  • Cite pre-registered, peer-reviewed findings
Researchers & Educators
Open datasets, codebooks, and a structured literature corpus for building on or teaching from.
  • Download coded SLR data with full variable documentation
  • Replicate or extend our exposure harmonization framework
  • Use our protocol as a template for your own SLR
Workers & Unions
Plain-language summaries of what the evidence says about which occupations face real risk — and which don't.
  • See exposure estimates for specific job categories
  • Understand the difference between augmentation and displacement
  • Participate in Wave 2 of our stakeholder survey
Journalists & Media
Citable, peer-reviewed findings and a research assistant trained on our corpus to answer questions quickly.
  • Ask FAIRWORK for sourced answers in seconds
  • Download data directly for data journalism
  • Request a background briefing from the research team
