Coding the research on AI and work

We code every eligible peer-reviewed study on AI and work across 27 dimensions, including the technology studied, its effects on job quality and the policies the authors recommend. The full database is open to everyone.

Overview

Mapping research on AI and work

About this project

Claims about AI and work are everywhere, but the research behind them is scattered across medicine, engineering, management, economics and education. This review gathers that research in one place and codes each study the same way, so patterns and blind spots become visible.

Undergraduate researchers at GRAIL screen and code every paper using a shared codebook. Each record captures what technology was studied, where and how, how the authors frame AI, which impacts on workers they examine, and what responses they recommend.

Research questions

  1. How does peer-reviewed research frame AI's effects on work: as a threat, an opportunity, or both?
  2. Which sectors, technologies, workers and outcomes does the evidence cover, and which does it leave out?
  3. What policy and design responses do researchers recommend, and how often?

Methods

How the dataset is built

Records come from Web of Science. Each is screened for whether it directly studies AI's effects on work; included papers are coded by trained undergraduate researchers, with a subsample coded independently by two people to check reliability.

Codebook dimensions

What this data can and can't tell you

  • It describes the published research, not the labor market. A sector with many papers is well studied, not necessarily most affected.
  • Attitude and framing codes reflect how authors present their findings, as judged by a coder, not effect sizes.
  • Screening is still underway, so counts and percentages will change as more papers are added.
  • "Unclear" is a common code: many papers don't address a given dimension at all. Charts of impacts and policy count only papers that do.
  • Reliability checks are in progress; agreement statistics will be published once enough double-coded papers are complete.
